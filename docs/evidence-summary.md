# Evidence supporting the required guidance

Source snapshots and hashes are recorded in docs/intake.json and input/evidence.json. Findings below use the supplied material only. Recommendations are source-derived summaries for a synthetic Connectathon artifact; no source organization endorses this implementation.

| Guidance | Supporting reported fall-rate result | Recommendation assessment | Important limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exercise | 29 trials, 14,475 participants; IRR 0.85, 95% CI 0.75 to 0.96; longest follow-up 6 to 24 months | USPSTF grade B; moderate certainty of moderate net benefit | Variable interventions and baseline risks; no one exercise regimen selected |
| Individualized multifactorial consideration | 20 trials, 22,115 participants; IRR 0.84, 95% CI 0.74 to 0.95; longest follow-up 6 to 28 months | USPSTF grade C; moderate certainty of small net benefit | Assessment and intervention components, adherence and implementation varied; not routine provision to everyone |

Locators: supplied USPSTF recommendation, **Benefits of Interventions**, **Harms of Interventions**, and **Table 1**; supplied AHRQ evidence-update snapshot, **Structured Abstract: Results/Limitations/Conclusions**. The AHRQ snapshot is an abstract and contents page, not the full book.

These are rates of falls, including repeat falls. They are not the number of people who fall, injurious falls, fractures, or mortality. The associated EvidenceVariables keep those meanings separate. Comparator roles are explicit in each Evidence record. The supplied summaries do not describe one uniform control regimen, so no such regimen is invented.

Harms were incompletely reported, usually minor musculoskeletal effects; rare serious events occurred. Sparse reporting is not proof of safety. Lack of a statistically significant result for other outcomes is not proof of no effect.

The supplied Pillay review uses different intervention categories and network meta-analysis methods. It separates purely multifactorial interventions from mixed interventions with components given to everyone. Its discussion explicitly explains why its findings need not match the USPSTF review's categories. We do not combine their estimates or use Pillay to change the required USPSTF B/C guidance. Its patient-preference findings support retaining an individualized decision, not choosing a mandatory delivery format. Locators: **Discussion: Comparison with other reviews and guidelines**, **Preferences for interventions**, **Limitations of the reviews**. Image-only tables unavailable in the supplied HTML snapshot are not claimed as independently inspected.

The CDC algorithm and pocket guide explain screening in a broader falls workflow. Only the track's three questions and two guidance actions are implemented. The CDC economics snapshot supplies background context, not a cost threshold or executable decision. Referenced external clinical documents and linked supplements were not incorporated.

R4 representation: two Evidence resources identify the two incident-fall-rate questions. Seven descriptive EvidenceVariables distinguish the two research populations, interventions, comparator, outcome and track population. Numerical findings remain attributed narrative; no R5 statistics fields or extra statistical resource family is introduced. These companions do not create patient observations or new eligibility rules.
